# Reformulating the financial statements

Goal: separate operating from financing so that operating income, NOA and free cash flow are clean. Do all four statements; they must tie together.

## Contents
1. Balance sheet
2. Income statement
3. Cash flow statement
4. Statement of shareholders' equity
5. Tricky items
6. Articulation checks

## 1. Balance sheet
Classify every line as operating or financing.
- Financial assets: cash held as a financial investment, short-term investments, interest-bearing securities, loans receivable of a financial nature.
- Financial obligations: borrowings, bonds, notes, finance leases, preferred stock (if treated as debt-like), interest-bearing items.
- Operating assets: receivables, inventory, PPE, intangibles, goodwill, operating cash needed for the business, equity-method investments in operating affiliates, deferred tax assets from operations.
- Operating liabilities: payables, accruals, deferred revenue, warranty reserves, deferred tax liabilities from operations, pension obligations (net, usually).
- Layout: NOA = operating assets - operating liabilities; NFO = financial obligations - financial assets; CSE = NOA - NFO. Minority interest and preferred stock sit between NFO and CSE in the equity claim stack; show them separately.
- "Strategic" balance sheet: group operating items by business activity (e.g. working capital, long-term operating assets), so margins and turnover can be tied to line items.

## 2. Income statement
- Operating income (OI) after tax = sales - operating expenses - tax on operating income. Include other operating income (equity-method income, restructuring, impairments) but tag unusual items separately.
- Net financial expense (NFE) = interest expense - interest income, after tax, plus financial gains/losses, preferred dividends if treated as financing.
- Tax allocation: tax on OI = reported tax expense + net interest expense x marginal rate (the interest tax shield is credited to financing, so remove it from operations). Taxes on unusual items at the marginal rate, shown separately.
- Comprehensive income = OI - NFE (then split minority interest). Include "dirty surplus" items (see below), otherwise ROCE and residual income are misstated.
- Separate core OI from sales, core other OI, and unusual items (analysis file).

## 3. Cash flow statement
- Operating cash flow C = reported CFO + after-tax net interest paid. Investing I = reported investing cash flow - net purchases of financial (interest-bearing) instruments. Free cash flow = C - I.
- Financing side: free cash flow is disposed of as net dividends to shareholders and net payments to debt holders and issuers. Reclassify items so debt flows and equity flows appear on the right side.
- Reclassify: acquisitions of operating businesses are investment in operations; purchases of financial securities are financing; interest paid moves out of CFO (after tax); stock-option exercise proceeds are share issues.
- Cross-check against the balance sheet: FCF = OI - change in NOA.

## 4. Statement of shareholders' equity
- Roll forward: beginning CSE + comprehensive income - net dividends (dividends + repurchases - issues) = ending CSE.
- Anything that changes CSE but is not in net income or net transactions with shareholders is dirty surplus: unrealized gains on securities, foreign currency translation, pension adjustments, derivative hedges, share-based compensation booked directly to equity. Under IFRS and US GAAP these often appear as other comprehensive income; check for those that do not.
- Hidden dirty surplus: shares issued in operations (e.g. stock compensation, acquisitions for stock) are value-neutral for the firm but must be shown as share issues, not income. Treat shares issued at fair value as a transaction with owners; do not let them inflate payout or earnings.
- Diluted EPS adjusts for options; for valuation, use basic shares plus an explicit option-overhang liability.

## 5. Tricky items
- Cash: needed operating cash is operating; excess is financial. In practice treat all cash as financial unless the business needs a working balance.
- Leases: operating leases are off-balance-sheet debt; capitalize them as financing (add to NOA and NFO) if the lease burden is material, and move implied interest out of operating expenses. Choose one treatment and keep it consistent across years and peers.
- Pensions: unfunded obligations after-tax can be treated as debt-like or as operating; state which, and be consistent. Pension service cost is operating; interest cost and expected return belong with financing if the liability is treated as debt.
- Goodwill and acquired intangibles: operating; keep them in NOA and be aware of impairment.
- Deferred taxes: usually operating; reclassify only if tied to financing items.
- Discontinued operations: either strip out of forecasts and value separately, or carry as a separate line; do not blend into core OI.
- Equity investments: operating affiliates stay in NOA (income in OI); passive stakes are financial.
- Banks and insurers: deposits and policy liabilities are operating; the standard split fails, handle with a financial-institution adaptation.

## 6. Articulation checks (must pass)
1. CSE (balance sheet) = beginning CSE + comprehensive income - net dividends.
2. Change in NOA = OI - FCF.
3. Change in NFO = NFE - FCF + net dividends.
4. FCF from cash flow reformulation = OI - change in NOA from the balance sheet (up to rounding).
5. Sum of reformulated income = reported comprehensive income (after the tax reallocation net effect).
If a check fails, find the missing item before analyzing. It is usually dirty surplus, a misclassified cash item, or tax allocation.
